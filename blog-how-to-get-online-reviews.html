Online Reviews: How Many Do You Need, and How Do You Get Them?

Local SEO September 12, 2026
  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. Online Reviews: How Many Do You Need, and How Do You Get Them?

You've probably heard that reviews build trust. True, but it's only half the story. Google also uses your reviews — how many you have, how recent they are, and how you respond to them — as a ranking signal for local search. Reviews aren't just word of mouth anymore. They're part of your SEO.

So how many do you actually need, and how do you get real customers to leave them without it feeling awkward? Let's go through what works.

Why review count and recency matter to Google

Think of Google Maps rankings like a popularity contest that never really ends. A business with 80 reviews, half of them from the last three months, looks active and trusted. A business with 12 reviews, all from two years ago, looks like it might not even be open anymore — even if it's thriving.

Google's local algorithm leans on signals like review quantity, review recency, and your average rating to help decide who shows up in the map pack for searches like "massage therapist near me." More recent reviews tend to carry more weight than old ones, which is exactly why a steady trickle of new reviews beats a big batch you got once and never followed up on.

How to actually get customers to write one

Most customers are happy to leave a review. They just rarely think to do it on their own. The fix isn't fancier software — it's asking at the right moment, in the easiest way possible.

  • Ask right after the positive moment — the end of a great appointment, not three days later in a generic email
  • Send a direct link straight to your Google review box, not just "search us on Google" — every extra step loses people
  • Make it personal: a text or email from a real name, not a no-reply blast
  • Respond to every review, good or bad — it shows future customers (and Google) that someone's actually paying attention

A direct link removes almost every excuse. The business that makes it a 15-second task gets reviews. The business that makes it a scavenger hunt doesn't.

Responding matters more than most owners realize. A short, genuine reply to a less-than-perfect review — acknowledging the issue, explaining what you'll do about it — often reassures future customers more than a wall of five-star reviews with silence underneath them. It shows you're present and you care, which is exactly the impression you want someone to have before they book.

How many is "enough"

There's no magic number, but a useful rule of thumb: aim to stay ahead of your closest local competitors, and aim for a handful of new reviews every month rather than a one-time push. Consistency beats volume — ten reviews spread evenly over a year outperform fifty that all arrived in one week and then stopped.

This is also exactly the kind of ongoing work that local SEO is built around — it isn't a one-time fix, it's steady upkeep that compounds over months.

What to do about a bad review

Every business gets one eventually, even a great one. The instinct is to panic or argue. Don't. Reply calmly, acknowledge what happened, and offer to make it right somewhere private — an email address or a request to reach out directly. Future customers read these exchanges closely, and a measured, professional response often does more for your reputation than the bad review did damage.

What you shouldn't do is ignore it and hope it sinks. Unanswered negative reviews sit at the top of your profile for anyone comparing you to the business down the street, and silence reads as indifference. A thoughtful reply, even a short one, closes that gap.

Reviews are one of the few marketing tools that get easier the longer you do them, because every good experience you deliver is a review waiting to be asked for. The habit of asking is the hard part — the writing is up to the customer.
