No-Show Rates: What's Normal, and How Booking Software Cuts Them

Operations October 17, 2026
  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. No-Show Rates: What's Normal, and How Booking Software Cuts Them

If one in every eight clients on your calendar simply doesn't show up, is that normal, or is something wrong? It's a fair question, and most small business owners have never seen a real number to compare themselves to — they just feel frustrated every time it happens.

So here's the benchmark: across service businesses like salons, spas, clinics and repair shops, a typical no-show rate runs somewhere between 10% and 20% of booked appointments. If you're inside that range, you're not failing — you're average. But average still costs real money, and it's fixable.

Why a 10-20% No-Show Rate Is the Norm

People forget. Life gets in the way. A client books three weeks out, something comes up, and your appointment quietly slips their mind. There's rarely malice in it — but the effect on your business is the same whether it's forgetfulness or indifference.

The reason the range is so wide is that it depends heavily on how far ahead people book and how they're reminded. A walk-in-heavy business with same-day bookings sees fewer no-shows. A business that books a week or more out, with no reminder system, tends to sit at the higher end — or above it.

What Actually Moves the Number Down

This is where booking software earns its keep. Automated reminders — a text or email sent a day or two before the appointment — consistently cut no-show rates by 30 to 50 percent. That alone can take a business sitting at 18% down closer to 10% or lower, without anyone lifting a finger to make a reminder call.

Deposits push the number down further. When a client has already put money down to hold a slot, walking away from it costs them something. Even a small deposit changes the math in their head from "I'll just skip it" to "I should at least call and reschedule."

  • Automated text and email reminders sent a day or two out
  • A small deposit or card-on-file required at booking
  • A simple one-tap reschedule link instead of making clients call
  • A cancellation window that's clearly stated up front

A 15% no-show rate isn't a character flaw in your clients — it's a system that's never been asked to do better.

None of this requires hiring anyone or spending your evenings on the phone. A modern booking system like Square Appointments handles reminders and deposit collection automatically, the moment it's set up — it just runs quietly in the background every day.

Tracking Your Own Number

Before you can fix a no-show problem, it helps to know your actual rate. Divide missed appointments by total booked appointments over a month, and you'll have your baseline. Most booking platforms calculate this for you automatically once you're using one.

Once you know where you stand, a drop from 18% to 10% isn't abstract — you can translate it directly into dollars, based on your average ticket size. That's usually the number that makes the case for switching systems obvious.

A no-show rate in the 10-20% range is common, but common doesn't mean you have to live with it. Reminders and deposits are proven, low-effort ways to bring that number down and keep your calendar full of appointments that actually happen.
