Google Business Profile Checklist: 12 Things Most Businesses Get Wrong

Local SEO August 29, 2026
  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. Google Business Profile Checklist: 12 Things Most Businesses Get Wrong

Most business owners set up their Google Business Profile once, years ago, and never touch it again. That's a problem, because Google treats it less like a listing and more like a living profile it keeps watching.

A profile that's complete, accurate and active tends to show up higher on Google Maps than one that's just technically there. Here's a practical checklist of the twelve things that make the biggest difference, in plain terms.

None of these require technical skill. Most take a few minutes each. The challenge is remembering to come back and do them, which is exactly why so many businesses let their profile quietly go stale.

The Basics Most People Skip

These are the fields that take five minutes each, and most businesses still have at least one wrong or missing.

  • Primary category matches exactly what you do — not a close-enough guess
  • Secondary categories added for every other service you genuinely offer
  • Hours are accurate, including holiday hours updated before the holiday, not after
  • Service area set correctly if you don't have a public storefront
  • Website and booking links point to live, working pages
  • Business description uses the words customers actually search, not marketing language

The Parts That Need Ongoing Attention

This is where most profiles fall behind, because it isn't a one-time setup — it's a habit.

Photos matter more than owners expect. A profile with recent, real photos of the work, the space or the team signals activity to both Google and the customer deciding whether to call. Stale photos from the day you opened do the opposite.

Think of your profile the way you'd think of a storefront window. A window with the same faded display for three years tells passersby nobody's minding the shop. A window that changes with the seasons tells them the business is alive and paying attention — and Google reads those same signals when it decides who to rank first.

Google Maps ranking isn't just about what's true on your profile — it's about what looks recently true.

  • New photos added at least monthly, not just once at setup
  • Google Posts used for updates, offers or seasonal news
  • Every review responded to — good and bad — within a few days
  • The Q&A section checked regularly; unanswered questions sit there publicly
  • Products or services listed with current, accurate pricing where possible
  • Messaging turned on and actually monitored, if you enable it

Why This Adds Up to More Than the Sum

No single item on this list will transform your ranking by itself. But Google is comparing your profile against every competitor in your area who's also trying to show up for the same search. If they've checked eight of these boxes and you've checked three, the gap shows up exactly where it matters: who appears first when a customer searches nearby.

It's also worth remembering that Google Maps results change based on where the searcher is standing and what they typed, so there's rarely a single "rank" to chase. The goal isn't to win every search everywhere — it's to make sure your profile gives Google every reason to show you to the customers who are actually close by and actually looking.

This is also one of the few places where consistent effort compounds. A profile that gets a little attention every week, instead of a lot of attention once a year, tends to pull ahead steadily.

It's also one of the few local SEO levers that doesn't cost anything to pull. Updating hours, replying to a review, or adding a new photo takes minutes and costs nothing — the barrier is almost entirely about remembering to do it on a schedule rather than letting weeks slide by.

Run through this list against your own profile honestly. Most businesses find at least three or four things worth fixing, and some of them take minutes.
