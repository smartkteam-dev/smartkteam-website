Insurance Billing Mistakes That Delay Massage Therapy Claims

Insurance August 22, 2026
  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. Insurance Billing Mistakes That Delay Massage Therapy Claims

A denied or delayed insurance claim rarely has one dramatic cause. It's almost always something small and fixable — a code, a missing form, a date — that slipped through before the claim was ever submitted.

For massage therapists who bill insurance, that small slip can mean weeks of waiting on a payment that should have arrived on time. Here are the mistakes that cause the most delays, and the simple habits that prevent them.

Mistake 1: Using the Wrong Billing Code

Every treatment you bill has to be matched to a specific code (a short number that tells the insurer exactly what service was performed). Use the wrong one, or a code that doesn't match what's written in your treatment notes, and the claim gets flagged or denied outright. It doesn't matter that the treatment was legitimate — if the paperwork doesn't line up, the insurer has no reason to pay.

Double-check codes against your actual session notes before you submit, every time, even for routine visits. A five-minute check beats a six-week resubmission.

It helps to keep a simple reference sheet of the codes you use most often, and to update it whenever a payer changes its requirements. Codes aren't universal across every insurer forever — they get revised, and a code that was accepted last year can be flagged this year if you haven't kept up.

Mistake 2: Not Verifying Coverage First

It's tempting to treat the client first and sort out insurance later. But if you never confirmed the client's plan actually covers massage therapy, or how many visits it covers per year, you can end up billing a claim that was never going to be paid in the first place.

  • Confirm active coverage before the first session, not after
  • Ask specifically whether massage therapy requires a referral or pre-authorization
  • Check the visit limit so you know when a client is approaching it

A claim that was never going to be covered isn't a billing mistake you can fix later — it's one you have to prevent before the first appointment.

Mistake 3: Missing or Incomplete Documentation

Insurers want proof the treatment was medically necessary, not just a line item. Vague notes — "massage, 60 minutes" with nothing else — invite a denial or a request for more information, which resets the clock on when you get paid.

Good documentation doesn't need to be long. It needs to answer the obvious question an adjuster will ask: why was this treatment necessary for this patient, on this date? A note that names the condition being treated, the areas worked, and how the patient responded covers most of what's required.

Mistake 4: Filing Late

Every insurer has a filing deadline, and it's shorter than most people expect — sometimes as little as 90 days from the date of service. Miss it, even by accident, and the claim can be denied no matter how correct everything else is. There's no partial credit for being almost on time.

Build claim submission into your weekly routine instead of batching it up for "whenever there's time." A small, steady habit avoids the pile-up that leads to missed deadlines.

None of these mistakes are complicated once you know to watch for them. They're just easy to miss when you're also running the rest of the business — booking clients, doing the actual treatments, answering emails.

That's really the core issue. Billing correctly isn't hard in the abstract. It's hard to do consistently, claim after claim, while also being the person who does the hands-on work. A single missed step on a busy week is enough to start a delay that takes far longer to untangle than it would have taken to prevent.
